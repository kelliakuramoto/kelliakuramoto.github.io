Deal Accelerator, for BizBuySell

BACKGROUND

BizBuySell is the largest online marketplace for buying and selling small businesses. Business brokers, who help owners sell their business, were stitching together email, DocuSign, Dropbox, and other tools to qualify buyers and share confidential files. Our goal was to move beyond a lead generation platform into a deal management system, ensuring broker and buyer activity stays on-platform.

TLDR

I led the design for Deal Accelerator, a two-sided deal management product that helps business brokers close business-for-sale deals, from marketing a listing and qualifying leads to sharing secure files and accepting offers. The experience spans four surfaces (Listing Dashboard, Lead Profile, Virtual Data Room, and Buyer Platform), giving brokers control of the process while guiding buyers through the sale proccess.

Listing Dashboard, for Brokers

I designed an entirely new surface to replace a single dropdown that previously managed all listing settings. Each listing now has its own dashboard, organized into four tabs: the Dashboard displays listing performance, insights, and next steps; Files is where brokers upload and manage data room files, Contacts shows what files have been shared with which leads and Settings is where brokers configure how leads engage with their listing during the qualification process.

Lead Profile, for Qualifying Buyers

I redesigned the lead experience from a single row in a table into a full profile. The new surface leverages all lead data, from NDA completion to Buyer Profile information and file activity, into clear signals of how serious they are, so brokers can view, disqualify, and take action on their leads from one place.

Virtual Data Room, for Secure File Sharing

I led the design for a listing-specific data room where brokers organize confidential documents and share them with qualified buyers. A key challenge was defining the privacy and sharing settings, so I made it clear who can see what and kept confidentiality front and center through Access Groups.

Deal Navigator, for Buyers

I redesigned the buyer experience from a single table with two buttons (Sign NDA and View CIM) to a guided experience for buyers with a task checklist. Buyers can see exactly which actions they need to take, access Virtual Data Room files, and request next steps from the broker, giving them more control over the process.

FINAL DESIGNS - SUPER FAN

FINAL DESIGNS - MID-CAREER MUSICIAN

I attacked the problem of Artist monetization from both sides of the customer experience: Artist and Fan. Balancing the needs of both customers was certainly a challenge throughout the entire summer. However, it was clear that both fans and artists wanted connection with each other (in one way or another).

THE PROBLEM

Artists aren’t able to earn a living solely based off streaming revenue

Fans feel like the Artist doesn’t engage with their community enough

So what do our customers actually need? Artists need a way to convert a streaming audience into purchasing customers. Fans need to anticipate and receive communication from the artists they love. These customer needs became the foundation for my problem statement for the summer.

HOW MIGHT WE

Use fan bundles to provide artists with financial stability and a growing fan community, while also strengthening fans' relationships with the artists they love?

I started off my summer with research. I knew that in order to define what could be, I had to first understand what currently is.

COMPETITIVE ANALYSIS

Analyzed 39+ companies across 11 different industries to gain a better understanding of what “product bundling” meant

Across multiple industries and companies, a common definition emerged. Product bundle: curated collection of complementary products or services for sale as one product or service package.

Throughout this research I also noted 3 high level advantages of product bundles:

  1. Notion of perceived price benefit
  2. Sense of scarcity or ephemerality
  3. Combination of items can create a unique whole

Once I had a clearer understanding of my problem space, I knew I had to validate my assumptions and gain actual customer perspective through generative research.

VALIDATING ASSUMPTIONS

Ran a card sort with 11 fans to uncover their mental models and preferences around bundles

The biggest takeaway was that true monetization required both personalization and range when it came to purchase conversions. One bundle won't work for every fan. Leveraging the insights from the card sort, as well as past UX research from my mentor Marvin, I developed three archetypes to guide my recommendations:

Conducted a survey with 7 artists to better understand what kinds of items and interactions they’d be open to

High level insights from both the research studies:

  1. Most fans placed physical merchandise in their top bundles
  2. 86% of artists said they'd be open to multiple types of fan interactions
  3. Fans were highly polarized with regards to participating in Artist interactions
  4. Fans who wanted high-engagement interactions were willing to spend 2x as much on a bundle

After analyzing what items and interactions artists were willing to offer and overlapping that with what fans were willing to pay, I used this data to inform my 4 different product bundle recommendations.

BUNDLE RECOMMENDATIONS

I designed around two customers: Dominic, a mid-career musician who's played local venues but can't support himself on streaming alone, and Lauren, one of his biggest fans, who has followed him since his first EP and even caught one of his early shows at Bill Graham Auditorium.

DESIGNING THE EXPERIENCE - SUPER FAN

Anticipation

Lauren gets a push notification that Dominic is releasing an exclusive fan bundle tomorrow. Tapping it opens a preview page where she can explore what's inside and add it to her Amazon List.

Release Day

She opens the Amazon Retail app, finds Dominic's merch shop, and sees the Fan Bundle front and center.

Customization

On the Retail product page, she chooses a vinyl album for her record player and a small t-shirt for his next concert.

Integrated Checkout

As a Prime member, checkout is seamless, and she lands in the Amazon Music app with everything in her Library.

Belonging

On Dominic's artist page she sees a personalized widget celebrating her purchase, and on the bundle detail page she can connect with other superfans through comments and share reactions in real time.

DESIGNING THE EXPERIENCE - MID-CAREER MUSICIAN

Easy Setup

In Amazon Music for Artists, he connects his Amazon Merch account and items auto-populate into curated bundle templates.

Customization

He can add or remove any items or interactions, and set his own price and quantity based on his monetization needs and bandwidth.

Track and Promote

Once live, he sees real-time stats on engagement and sales, can embed a promo widget on his personal website, and can use Amazon Music's other promo tools like fan reach and promo cards.
